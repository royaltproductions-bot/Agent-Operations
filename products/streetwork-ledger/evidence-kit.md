# Streetwork Ledger — Construction Disruption Evidence Kit
Version 0.1 | 2026-10-10 | Independent research and original worksheet

## Purpose
Help a storefront affected by nearby roadwork assemble a dated, auditable record of sales comparisons, access conditions, and possible contributing factors. This kit is a documentation aid, not a promise of grants, damages, compensation, or insurance coverage. No government affiliation is claimed.

## Evidence collection — retain originals
1. Weekly POS reports: gross sales, refunds, discounts, sales tax, and order counts, using the same basis for both periods.
2. Comparable baseline weeks (prefer the same week of the prior year); document holidays, promotions, price changes, and seasonal effects.
3. Date-stamped photographs of street access, sidewalk access, parking availability, delivery restrictions, and signage.
4. Official road closure advisories and notices, retaining dates and original URLs.
5. Customer cancellations, missed deliveries, reduced hours, and written observations with dates. Do not invent counterfactual customer visits.
6. Payroll, rent, utilities, and other fixed expenses separately. Do not equate gross-sales variance with lost profit.
7. A chronology linking each factual observation to the corresponding week and source document.

## Weekly comparison worksheet
Create one row per week with these fields:

| Week starting | Baseline gross sales USD | Actual gross sales USD | Variance USD | Access condition | Supporting file(s) | Other explanations / limitations |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-07 | 3000.00 | 1600.00 | -1400.00 | Parking restricted | EXAMPLE_photo_01.jpg | FICTIONAL EXAMPLE; other causes not assessed |
| 2026-09-14 | 3200.00 | 1800.00 | -1400.00 | Road closed | EXAMPLE_notice_02.pdf | FICTIONAL EXAMPLE; weather unknown |

Formula: weekly variance = actual gross sales minus baseline gross sales. Percentage variance = weekly variance divided by baseline, if baseline is greater than zero. Sum weekly dollar variances only when weeks are comparable. A negative variance is not proof of causation.

## Review controls

- Separate estimates, observations, and documentary facts.
